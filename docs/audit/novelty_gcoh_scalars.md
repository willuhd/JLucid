# Novelty check: dictionary-free LEiDA scalars "gcoh" (mean |λ1|/N) and "gcoh_sd" (temporal SD)

Literature-novelty verification note, compiled via Exa web search (Cabral/Deco/Kringelbach LEiDA lineage + ADHD phase-synchrony literature).
Definition of our scalars: per frame t, take the cosine phase-coherence (phase-locking) matrix PC(t); compute its leading eigenvalue λ1(t); gcoh = mean over frames of |λ1(t)|/N_rois; gcoh_sd = temporal SD of |λ1(t)|/N_rois.
Findings: ADHD dx (ADHD-200 NYU, n=257): higher gcoh and gcoh_sd (t=+2.9, p~.002–.005, covariates age/sex/FD, permutation p). HBN (n=790): attention-symptom factor ↔ gcoh_sd (p=.0018, rho~.10), marginal gcoh.

---

## 1. Direct precedent for the scalars — YES for the quantities, NO for their use as human clinical/psychopathology subject metrics

**Short answer: the two quantities have a direct published methodological precedent (EiDA, 2024), but they have never been used as dictionary-free subject-level metrics in a human clinical study (no ADHD, no psychiatric group/symptom application). The LEiDA mainstream reports occupancy/dwell of the *global state* (related but discrete), and the *phase-angle* Kuramoto synchrony/metastability pair (healthy only), not the eigenvalue fraction.**

### What the core LEiDA papers do with λ1
- **Cabral et al. 2017 (Sci Rep 7:5135)** — the founding LEiDA paper. It explicitly notes the leading eigenvector reduces dimensionality "while still explaining most of its variance (see Supplemental Figure S2)". The eigenvalue itself is **not analyzed**; all subject metrics are fractional occupancy, lifetime/dwell, and switching probabilities of clustered states. State #1 ("global coherence", all same-sign V1) occurs >40% of the time and is linked to the "global signal".
- **Farinha, Amado, Morgado & Cabral 2022 (Front Neurosci 16:821179, "Increased Excursions to Functional Networks in Schizophrenia in the Absence of Task")** — reports "the leading eigenvector accounted for more than 50% of the variance in phase coherence at all time points and for all subjects", but again only as a *validation* of the dimensionality reduction. Group metrics = occupancy/dwell/limiting probability of the Global Mode and network states.
- **Vohryzek et al. 2020 (Front Syst Neurosci 14:20, "Ghost Attractors...")** — formalizes occupancy/dwell/transition metrics; the globally synchronized state is the high-dwell "meta-stable globally synchronized" baseline; global state shows high between-subject variability. No eigenvalue metric.
- No LEiDA paper found that uses mean(λ1) or SD(λ1) — or any per-frame eigenvalue summary — as a **subject-level scalar** or in a group comparison.

### The direct methodological precedent: EiDA (de Alteriis et al., 2024, Imaging Neuroscience)
**de Alteriis G, MacNicol E, Hancock F, Ciaramella A, Cash D, Expert P, Turkheimer FE (2024). "EiDA: A lossless approach for dynamic functional connectivity; application to fMRI data of a model of ageing." *Imaging Neuroscience* 2(2). doi:10.1162/imag_a_00113** (preprint: bioRxiv 2023.02.27.529688).
EiDA exploits the fact that the cosine phase-locking matrix is **rank 2** with λ1 + λ2 = N (trace), and explicitly defines, "based on the norm of the iPL matrix":
- **"spectral radius" = first eigenvalue λ1 = the norm of the instantaneous phase-locking matrix** ("position" in their Continuous-EiDA position–speed embedding) — **mathematically our per-frame quantity** (ours is the normalized version λ1/N).
- **"spectral metastability" = the standard deviation over time of the spectral radius (λ1)** — **mathematically identical in definition to our gcoh_sd**.
Their application: longitudinal **rat** fMRI (48 Sprague-Dawley rats × 4 ages, 44 regions, 9.4T, isoflurane). Findings with ageing: total connectivity (λ1-based) **decreased** (29.5±1.8 → 27.0±1.0, ANOVA p≈1e-8); **spectral metastability decreased** (3.2±0.4 → 2.6±0.5, p≈1e-6); reconfiguration speed of the first eigenvector increased; informational complexity increased and was **inversely correlated with the mean first eigenvalue (R = −0.95, p < 0.001)**. They also showed SD(λ1) relates to classic Kuramoto metastability via a quadratic fit (adj R² ≈ 0.59) — i.e., gcoh_sd is a spectral analogue of metastability, correlated with but not identical to SD(Kuramoto order parameter R(t)). Note: for the rank-2 PSD cosine matrix λ1/N ∈ [0.5, 1], so gcoh is bounded and interpretable as the fraction of phase-coupling "energy" in the dominant (global) mode.
**Crucially, EiDA was a methods/aging-rats paper; the authors explicitly say the metrics are "promising for studies on neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative disorders" — i.e., the human clinical use was flagged as future work and has not been done (no human EiDA application found, including via their GitHub repo).**

### The conceptual (non-eigenvalue) precedent: phase-angle synchrony + metastability pair
- **Hancock F, Cabral J, Luppi AI, Rosas FE, Mediano PAM, Dipasquale O, Turkheimer FE (2022). "Metastability, fractal scaling, and synergistic information processing: What phase relationships reveal about intrinsic brain activity." *NeuroImage* 259:119433.** Uses LEiDA modes and computes **global synchronization = mean over time of the Kuramoto order parameter** and **global metastability = its SD**, in healthy young adults (4 runs, reliability only; no group/symptom comparisons). Directly relevant: **synchrony correlates r = 0.84 with occupancy of global mode 1** (and r = 0.92 with a phase-coherence coefficient) — confirming that a "global coherence level" scalar and global-state occupancy are near-redundant measures.
- **Deco & Kringelbach 2016 (Trends Neurosci 39:125)** and the Kuramoto metastability literature (Lee et al. 2018 Sci Rep "Linking functional connectivity and dynamic properties of resting-state networks": synchrony = mean R(t), metastability = SD R(t)) — the mean/SD-of-synchrony pair is standard, but always phase-angle-based, network-level, and (in humans) healthy or schizophrenia (Deco et al. 2017/2018), never ADHD.
- **Olsen et al. 2024 (bioRxiv 2024.11.15.623830, "Uncovering dynamic human brain phase coherence networks")** — methodological critique noting LEiDA "discards a portion of the data variance" by keeping only the leading eigenvector; motivates eigenvalue-aware modeling. Supports the interest in the eigenvalue fraction; no clinical scalar.

### Closest 5 papers (exact titles, what they measured, direction)
1. **de Alteriis et al. (2024), "EiDA: A lossless approach for dynamic functional connectivity; application to fMRI data of a model of ageing", Imaging Neuroscience 2(2).** — Measured: spectral radius (λ1) mean and **spectral metastability = SD(λ1)** per recording (≡ gcoh/gcoh_sd, unnormalized). Direction: rat ageing → ↓ total connectivity, ↓ spectral metastability, ↑ informational complexity (complexity ↔ mean λ1: R = −0.95). **Same quantities; animal/aging only; no clinical or human use.**
2. **Farinha et al. (2022), "Increased Excursions to Functional Networks in Schizophrenia in the Absence of Task", Front Neurosci 16:821179.** — Measured: LEiDA fractional occupancy & dwell time of the **globally synchronized state (Global Mode)**. Direction: **schizophrenia < controls** (occupancy 29.8% vs 39.8%, Hedges g=0.69; dwell 4.99 s vs 6.57 s, g=0.62); increased occupancy of anti-phase network states. Precedent for "global-coherence occupancy as a clinical metric", opposite sign to our ADHD finding.
3. **Hancock et al. (2022), "Metastability, fractal scaling, and synergistic information processing...", NeuroImage 259:119433.** — Measured: global synchrony (mean R), global metastability (SD R) from LEiDA phase-locking modes; reliability across runs; synchrony ↔ occupancy of global mode r = 0.84. Direction: healthy only (no group differences).
4. **Shappell, Duffy, Rosch, Pekar, Mostofsky, Lindquist, Cohen (2021), "Children with attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder spend more time in hyperconnected network states and less time in segregated network states as revealed by dynamic connectivity analysis", NeuroImage 229:117753.** — Measured: HSMM state dwell/sojourn on rs-fMRI (38 ADHD vs 79 TD children). Direction: **ADHD > TD: more time in the hyperconnected state (p<0.001), less in DMN-anticorrelated states** — correlation-based but directionally matching our gcoh↑ in ADHD.
5. **Guo H, Liu Y, Li Y, Guo Q-L, Hao Z-P, Yang Y, et al. (2025), "Self-organizing dynamic research based on phase coherence graph autoencoders: Analysis of brain metastable states across the lifespan", NeuroImage (PMID 40049301).** — Measured: LEiDA-based (graph-autoencoder) metastable-state indicators across the lifespan. Direction: **"Global metastable state shows a linear decline with age"** (whole-brain/global indicator; younger = higher), plus linear+quadratic age effects on state trajectory indicators.
(Honorable mentions: **Cabral et al. 2017 Sci Rep** — poorer cognition in older adults ↔ lower occupancy/dwell of global coherence state #1; **Cai et al. 2018, Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging** — ADHD children have "less persistent and more volatile brain states"; **Cabral et al. 2022 eLife**, "Fine-grained analysis of functional connectivity dynamics links cognitive performance in healthy aging to spontaneous switching between brain states".)

## 2. LEiDA-ADHD prior work — NONE found

No published LEiDA (leading-eigenvector phase-coherence) ADHD paper was found via multiple targeted searches ("LEiDA ADHD", "leading eigenvector dynamics attention deficit/hyperactivity", LEiDA in psychiatric/neurodevelopmental populations, and checks of the Cabral/Kringelbach/Deco code repositories and review citations). Documented LEiDA applications as of early 2026: healthy aging & cognition (Cabral 2017 Sci Rep; Cabral 2022 eLife), major depression (Figueroa 2019 HBM; Alonso-Martínez 2020 Front Neural Circuits; **Alonso et al. 2023 HBM, doi:10.1002/hbm.26475**, depression recurrence: longer DMN periods, more frequent attentional/reward states — no global-coherence scalar), schizophrenia (Farinha 2022), autism (Wang/Lu/Lin/Tian 2023 Front Integr Neurosci 16:922577 — 5 network states, occupancy altered in ASD; ABIDE-I n=945 LEiDA subtype paper, medRxiv 2025.01.29.25321316; Gao et al. 2026 Chaos, sex/age heterogeneity of LEiDA dFC in ASD), Parkinson (Kim et al. 2017 Brain 140:2955–2967 — sliding-window dFNC precursor, not phase-coherence LEiDA: PD patients shift toward the strongly interconnected State II and show higher variability of global efficiency), Alzheimer's (Yang et al. 2024 J Neurophysiol 132:744), psilocybin (Lord 2019), sleep/anesthesia (Deco 2019 PNAS), rat neurodevelopment & aging (França et al. 2022; EiDA 2024), preadolescents (Fasano et al. 2022 HBM, music LEiDA in 10–11-year-olds; ABCD n≈11,000 9–10-year-olds LEiDA+functional-connectome-harmonics preprint), irritability paradigm (Wellcome Open Research 2023), lifespan (Guo 2025 NeuroImage), and aging human LEiDA (Tang J, Wang Y, Xu X, Pan X, Wang R (2026), "Reorganization of Global Phase Pattern in Complex Brain Network During Aging Revealed by Leading Eigenvector Dynamics Analysis", *Int J Bifurcation & Chaos*, doi:10.1142/s0218127426501063 — 32 younger vs 28 older; visual-network PC states ↓ in elderly, subcortical ↑; no global-state eigenvalue scalar).
**Conclusion: ADHD is a conspicuous gap in the LEiDA literature. Our study would be the first LEiDA-lineage ADHD finding — and the first ADHD use of any leading-eigenvalue scalar.** (Caveat: an unpublished preprint could still exist; recommend a final PubMed/Google Scholar sweep before submission.)

## 3. ADHD global-synchrony / metastability direction in the literature — mostly HIGHER synchrony & higher volatility in ADHD (consistent with gcoh↑, gcoh_sd↑), with one caveat line

Evidence FOR higher global synchrony/order or higher temporal volatility in ADHD:
- **Shappell et al. 2021 (NeuroImage 229:117753, HSMM)**: ADHD children spend **significantly more time in a hyperconnected state** (p<0.001) and less time in anticorrelated/segregated states. Closest state-level analogue of "more global coherence".
- **Yu et al. (2013), "Additional Brain Functional Network in Adults with Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder", PLoS ONE 8(1):e54516** (fMRI phase-synchrony networks): phase-synchrony degree is **statistically enhanced in ADHD**; the added PS network increases global and local efficiency.
- **EEG / coupled-oscillator line (Jafari group and others)**: "ADHD brain networks exhibit higher synchronization compared to healthy controls" (Hindmarsh-Rose model on EEG networks, 2022–2025); abnormal (disorganized) synchronization-transition patterns in frontal regions in ADHD vs more goal-directed synchronization build-up in controls ("Transition to synchronization in functional brain networks of children suffering from ADHD"); Ansarinasab et al. 2022, J Theor Biol 560:111381 (PS in ADHD children via nonlinear recurrence measure). MEG criticality: ADHD severity predicted by shifts of the brain's operating point **toward the supercritical (more ordered/synchronous) side** ("ADHD symptoms across the population are explained by individual variability in brain criticality", Transl Psychiatry, doi:10.1038/s41398-026-04267-0).
- **Temporal volatility**: Cai et al. 2018 (Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging; SN-CEN-DMN dynamics, n=140 per group): ADHD children show **"less persistent and more volatile brain states"**; Michael et al. 2025 (Biol Psychiatry Cogn Neurosci Neuroimaging, doi:10.1016/j.bpsc.2024.11.006): ADHD children show **greater dynamic connectivity (sdRSS)** within/across salience, DMN, somatomotor networks; seed-to-voxel sliding-window on ADHD-200 (arXiv:2302.07961): **higher temporal variability of dFC in ADHD**. All consistent with gcoh_sd↑.
Caveats / conflicts to handle carefully:
- **Neural-flexibility line**: Yin W, Li T, Mucha PJ, Cohen JR, Zhu H, et al. (2022, *Molecular Psychiatry*, doi:10.1038/s41380-022-01706-4): children with ADHD show **decreased whole-brain neural flexibility** (multilayer modularity; medication normalizes it), citing also reduced FC temporal variability (Rolls et al.) in ADHD. This is a *module-membership switching* metric, not a global-mode fluctuation — but reviewers may see "reduced variability in ADHD" vs our "increased gcoh_sd" as a tension. Frame gcoh_sd explicitly as **fluctuation of the global (dominant-mode) coherence amplitude**, a Kuramoto-metastability-like quantity, not node/module flexibility.
- **Within-LEiDA direction precedents point the other way for other disorders**: schizophrenia ↓ global-mode occupancy (Farinha 2022); poorer cognition in older adults ↔ ↓ global-state occupancy (Cabral 2017); whereas vulnerable remitted MDD show ↑ time in a globally active network and MDD is described as "more pronounced synchronization and temporal stability" (Figueroa 2019; Vohryzek et al. 2022, Phil Trans R Soc A 380:20210247 review). Our ADHD direction (↑ gcoh) therefore aligns with the depression-like "excess global coherence" pole, not with the schizophrenia/aging "loss of global coherence" pole.
- Whole-brain modeling in ADHD (Kaboodvand/Iravani et al. 2021, NeuroImage 231:117844; bioRxiv 2020.05.08.084491): ADHD subgroups show **higher global bifurcation parameter** (stronger local oscillation amplitude) and lower coupling in one subgroup — indirect support for a shift toward a more self-sustained/ordered regime.
- No study was found that directly reports whole-brain mean phase synchrony (or SD) in ADHD vs TD with the Kuramoto order parameter on fMRI — that specific gap strengthens our novelty claim.

## 4. Developmental trends

- **Cabral 2017 (Sci Rep)**: in older adults, the **global coherence state (state #1, >40% occurrence)** is the metric of interest; **poorer cognitive performers show lower occurrence/dwell of the global state**, and good performers show higher probability of being in / returning to it. So "less global state = worse cognition" in aging. Our ADHD direction (higher gcoh with worse attention) is the **opposite** of that cognitive-performance mapping — do not analogize ADHD to cognitive decline; analogize to development/excitability instead.
- **Tang et al. 2026 (Int J Bifurcation & Chaos)**: LEiDA in younger vs older adults: elderly show reduced occurrence/lifetime of visual-network PC states and enhanced subcortical synchrony; no global-coherence scalar reported.
- **EiDA rat ageing (2024)**: **spectral metastability (SD λ1) declines with age** (3.2→2.6) and total connectivity (mean λ1) declines with age — i.e., **younger = higher mean λ1 and higher SD(λ1)**. This is the closest published analogue of our observation that younger children show higher global-coherence level/fluctuation — directionally consistent, but animal and healthy.
- **Guo et al. 2025 (NeuroImage, PCGAE/LEiDA lifespan)**: **global metastable-state indicator declines linearly with age** in humans — again younger = higher global-metastable engagement; consistent with our developmental covariance (younger kids = more gcoh fluctuation).
- **Correlation-based dFC development literature**: Rashid et al. 2018 (Generation R, n=774, HMM): older children spend **more time in a "globally modularized" state**, younger children more in globally disconnected states; López-Vicente et al. 2021 (Generation R longitudinal 8→15y): **number of state transitions decreases with age** (younger = more switching) and inter-network variability increases with age. Both are compatible with younger children showing higher gcoh_sd.
- **PLOS Computational Biology 2026 (EEG lifespan, n=111, 9–75 y)**: phase-synchrony switching dynamics follow an **inverted-U**: young adults slowest/most diverse switching; **children and older adults faster, more stereotyped dynamics** — a partial tension to note if we claim "higher fluctuation in young = healthier".
- LEiDA in children per se exists (Fasano 2022, 10–11 y; ABCD 9–10 y preprint; ASD LEiDA with age-stratified analyses, Gao 2026), but **no study reports developmental trends of global-state occupancy or eigenvalue-based global coherence in typically developing children/adolescents** — a citable gap.

## 5. Novelty verdict for gcoh / gcoh_sd

The two scalars are mathematically well-grounded (λ1 is the spectral radius of a rank-2 PSD cosine phase-locking matrix with λ1+λ2=N, so gcoh=λ1/N ∈ [0.5,1] is the fraction of phase-coupling captured by the dominant mode) and — this is the key precedent — they are **formally identical to two metrics already defined in EiDA (de Alteriis et al., 2024, Imaging Neuroscience): the "spectral radius/position" (λ1) and "spectral metastability" (SD of λ1)**. However, EiDA introduced those metrics as a computational shortcut and applied them only to longitudinal rat fMRI during ageing, explicitly deferring clinical use; and the conceptually adjacent pair (mean Kuramoto synchrony and metastability, Hancock et al. 2022) has only been reported in healthy adults. **What would be genuinely new in our work is therefore not the formulas but the application and the result: (i) the first human, clinical/psychopathological use of dictionary-free leading-eigenvalue global-coherence scalars; (ii) the first ADHD application of the entire LEiDA lineage — no published LEiDA-ADHD paper was found; (iii) a replication across two independent datasets (ADHD-200 dx and HBN symptom factor) of a symptom-linked global-coherence fluctuation index (gcoh_sd).** The direction (more ADHD → higher mean global coherence level and higher fluctuation of the leading mode) is *consistent* with the nearest ADHD literature — HSMM "hyperconnected-state" over-occupancy in ADHD children (Shappell 2021), enhanced fMRI phase-synchrony networks (Yu 2013), EEG/oscillator findings of higher synchronization, MEG supercritical shifts, and several reports of more volatile brain states (Cai 2018; Michael 2025) — and with the EiDA/lifespan observation that younger (less mature) brains show higher mean λ1 and higher SD(λ1). It is *not consistent* with the intuition imported from other LEiDA clinical papers that disease = less global coherence (schizophrenia: Farinha 2022; poor aging cognition: Cabral 2017) — so we should explicitly position ADHD as the "excess global phase organization" pole (depression-like, per Figueroa 2019/Vohryzek 2022), and pre-empt the neural-flexibility literature (Yin 2022: ADHD children have *reduced* module-switching flexibility) by stressing that gcoh_sd indexes fluctuation of the *global-mode amplitude* (a metastability-like quantity), not network/module flexibility. Finally, because gcoh is a near-redundant, continuous surrogate of global-state occupancy (synchrony ↔ global-mode occupancy r = 0.84 in Hancock 2022) and of the global signal (Cabral 2017 links state #1 to the global signal), reviewers should expect: (a) strong association between gcoh and global-state occupancy/global-signal amplitude — present it as a feature (dictionary-free, threshold/clustering-free continuum) and report correlations; (b) sensitivity to head motion and acquisition parameters — the FD/age/sex covariates and the two-cohort replication are the right defenses, and per-frame λ1/N is bounded ([0.5,1]) which helps interpretability. Bottom line: **novel in population, disorder, and link to symptoms; not novel as raw quantities (EiDA, rat aging, 2024) — cite EiDA prominently as the mathematical antecedent and frame ours as its first human psychopathology deployment, with ADHD being the first-ever LEiDA application in that disorder.**
